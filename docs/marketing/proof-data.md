# Proof data for the landing page

Sourced statistics supporting the value of tailoring a resume to a job, optimizing
for ATS, and using cover letters.

**Principle: accuracy over impressiveness.** Every number below is traced to a
real, named source with a year and URL. Where a popular stat turned out to be
unreliable or unsourced, it is flagged and excluded — do not publish those.

Last verified: 2026-06 (via web search; primary sources triangulated across
multiple outlets where the original was not directly reachable).

---

## Industry data (cited — safe to display)

These come from named studies/surveys with disclosed (or partially disclosed)
methodology. They are about the **industry**, not about our product. Always
attribute on the page. None of these is a claim about results our tool produces.

### 1. ~98% of Fortune 500 companies use an ATS

- **Claim:** Nearly all of the largest US companies screen applications through an
  applicant tracking system, so resumes must be machine-readable.
- **Number:** 98.4% of Fortune 500 companies used a detectable ATS (492 of 500).
- **Source:** Jobscan, 2024 ATS Usage Report (figure reported for 2024; the same
  report shows 97.4% in 2023 and ~97.8% in 2025 — consistently ~98%).
- **Year:** 2024.
- **URL:** https://www.jobscan.co/blog/fortune-500-use-applicant-tracking-systems/
- **Reliability:** High. Jobscan reverse-engineers each Fortune 500 career page to
  detect the ATS in use, so it measures actual deployment rather than survey
  recall. Caveat: "uses an ATS" is not the same as "auto-rejects resumes" — see
  the rejected myth below. Safe to phrase as "98% of Fortune 500 companies use an
  applicant tracking system," not "98% of resumes are auto-rejected."

### 2. Recruiters spend about 7 seconds on an initial resume scan

- **Claim:** A resume gets only seconds of attention, so relevance must be obvious
  at a glance.
- **Number:** 7.4 seconds average initial screening time (up from ~6 seconds in
  the 2012 version of the study).
- **Source:** Ladders, "Eye-Tracking Study" (2018 update of its 2012 study),
  widely reported by HR Dive and HR Daily Advisor.
- **Year:** 2018.
- **URL:** https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/
  (Ladders PDF: https://www.theladders.com/static/images/basicSite/pdfs/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf)
- **Reliability:** Medium. Real eye-tracking study from a known company and widely
  cited, but the published report does not disclose participant count, industries,
  or resume sample size, so treat it as directional/illustrative rather than
  precise. Cite as "about 7 seconds (Ladders, 2018)" — the rounded "7 seconds"
  framing is the most defensible. Avoid implying it is a large peer-reviewed study.

### 3. A comprehensive, tailored profile gets 71% more callbacks

- **Claim:** Putting effort into matching your application to the role
  measurably increases interview callbacks; a thin, generic application does not.
- **Number:** 13.5% callback rate for resumes linking a comprehensive LinkedIn
  profile vs 7.9% with no profile — 71% higher. (A "bare-bones" profile got 7.2%,
  i.e. no lift — effort is what moves the number.)
- **Source:** ResumeGo field experiment (24,570 fictitious resumes submitted to
  real openings on Indeed, ZipRecruiter, Glassdoor, Oct 2018–Mar 2019). Reported
  by Fortune and charted by Statista.
- **Year:** 2019.
- **URL:** https://www.resumego.net/research/linkedin-interview-chances/
  (Fortune: https://fortune.com/2019/03/28/job-applicants-with-a-comprehensive-linkedin-profile-71-more-likely-to-get-interviews-study-says/)
- **Reliability:** High for an industry source. Large, randomized field experiment
  with a control group; variables held constant across groups. Caveat: it
  measures the effect of a *comprehensive/effortful* profile, not literally
  "tailoring a resume." Phrase honestly, e.g. "Putting real effort into your
  application drove 71% more callbacks in a 24,570-resume field study" — do not
  restate it as "tailored resumes get 71% more callbacks."

### 4. The average corporate job opening draws ~250 resumes; ~4–6 get interviewed

- **Claim:** Competition is fierce, so standing out for the specific role matters.
- **Number:** 250 resumes per corporate opening; only 4–6 candidates interviewed,
  one hired.
- **Source:** Glassdoor (HR & recruiting statistics).
- **Year:** Glassdoor figure, widely cited (originally ~2015, repeated by Glassdoor
  since).
- **URL:** https://www.glassdoor.com/blog/50-hr-recruiting-stats-make-think/
- **Reliability:** Medium. From a reputable platform and ubiquitous in the
  industry, but the underlying dataset/date is not clearly documented and the
  figure is now several years old. Use as context ("a typical corporate opening
  attracts ~250 applicants"), not as a precise current measurement. Consider
  pairing with a newer volume stat if a stronger source is found.

### 5. Most hiring managers still read — and expect — cover letters

- **Claim:** Cover letters are not dead; a tailored one is expected and can tip a
  decision.
- **Numbers (ResumeGenius survey of 625 US hiring managers):**
  - 83% of hiring managers read cover letters.
  - 94% say cover letters influence their interview decisions.
  - 72% expect a cover letter even when the posting says it is optional.
  - 49% say a strong cover letter can earn an interview on its own.
- **Source:** ResumeGenius Cover Letter Statistics (hiring-manager survey, n=625,
  fielded 2023, updated through 2025/2026).
- **Year:** 2023 survey, refreshed 2025.
- **URL:** https://resumegenius.com/blog/cover-letter-help/cover-letter-statistics
- **Reliability:** Medium-high. Disclosed sample size (625) and respondent screening;
  vendor-run survey (ResumeGenius sells resume tools), so there is a mild interest
  bias toward "cover letters matter." Numbers are self-reported preferences, not
  observed behavior. Pick ONE headline (recommend 83% read them, or "94% say they
  influence interview decisions") and attribute clearly.

## Claims that need our OWN product data (do NOT publish as fact)

These are circulating widely but are either debunked, unsourced, or specifically
about a product's own results. Do not present any of them as cited industry fact.

### A. "75% of resumes are rejected by the ATS before a human sees them" — REJECTED / MYTH

- **Why excluded:** Traced to a **2012 sales pitch by Preptel**, a resume-optimization
  vendor that went out of business in 2013. No study, survey, or methodology was
  ever published. Investigations (e.g. career consultant Christine Assaf; recruiter
  Jan Tegze) found no academic support, and recruiters report that the large
  majority of applications are reviewed by a human. **Do not use this stat, or any
  "X% never seen by a human" variant.** It is the single most common false claim in
  this space and citing it undermines credibility.
- **Reference (debunk):** https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/ats-resume-rejection-myth/
- **Safe alternative:** Use stat #1 ("98% of Fortune 500 use an ATS") — true and
  defensible — instead of any auto-rejection percentage.

### B. "Tailored resumes get 40% / 50% / 2x more callbacks" — NEEDS OUR OWN DATA

- **Why flagged:** These specific figures (40% more callbacks, 50% more callbacks,
  "2x") appear across many resume-tool blogs (Jobscan, Wellfound's "78%", various
  AI-resume vendors) but each traces back to another blog or to unpublished
  internal vendor data, not to a reproducible, independent study. The directional
  claim ("tailoring helps") is well supported; the *exact percentages* are not
  reliably sourced. If we want a number like this on the page, it should come from
  **our own measured A/B / cohort data** and be labeled as our result. Until then,
  rely on stat #3 (ResumeGo's 71%, which is a real experiment, but about
  profile-completeness, not literal tailoring).

### C. "11.7% vs 4.2% callback for ATS-optimized vs generic (15,000 applications)" — NEEDS VERIFICATION / OUR OWN DATA

- **Why flagged:** A compelling specific stat, but it surfaces only in vendor blogs
  ("a study of 15,000 applications") with no identifiable, citable primary source.
  Do not publish until/unless a real primary source is located, or replace with our
  own product data.

### D. "Candidates with tailored cover letters are 1.9x more likely to land interviews" — NEEDS VERIFICATION

- **Why flagged:** Repeated in cover-letter listicles without a traceable original
  study. Treat as unverified. The ResumeGenius survey numbers in stat #5 are the
  defensible cover-letter facts.

### E. "60% of hiring managers consider quantifiable results most important (Jobvite)" — USE WITH CAUTION

- **Why flagged:** Attributed to Jobvite but the specific report/year is not clearly
  pinned in current sources. Verify the exact Jobvite Recruiter Nation report and
  year before publishing; otherwise omit.
