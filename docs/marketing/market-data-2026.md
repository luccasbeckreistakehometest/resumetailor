# Market data 2026 — sourced stats for ResumeTailor

Recent, citable statistics supporting the value of **tailoring / customizing** a
resume + cover letter to a specific job, ATS reality, and recruiter behavior.

**Principle: accuracy over impressiveness.** Every number is traced to a named
source with a year and URL. Where a popular figure traces only to vendor blogs
with no rigorous study, it is flagged below and must NOT be published as fact.

**These are INDUSTRY statistics, not measurements of our product's results.**
Never relabel them as "our results." Always show the source attribution.

Last verified: **2026-06** (via web search; primary sources triangulated across
multiple outlets where the original page was not directly reachable). This file
supersedes `proof-data.md` for the recency-prioritized set; `proof-data.md` is
retained for the longer reliability write-ups it still covers.

Recency legend: **[2024–2026]** = genuinely recent · **[older, still valid]** =
real study but pre-2024 · **[undated/aging]** = real-ish but no clear date.

## Cited & safe to display (with year)

Named studies/surveys with disclosed (or partially disclosed) methodology.
About the **industry**, not our tool. Always attribute on the page.

### 1. ~98% of Fortune 500 companies use an ATS  — **[2024–2026]**

- **Claim:** Nearly all of the largest US employers screen applications through an
  applicant tracking system, so resumes must be machine-readable.
- **Number:** **97.8% in 2025** (489 of 500). Year-by-year from the same report:
  97.4% (2023), 98.4% (2024), 97.8% (2025) — consistently ~98%.
- **Source:** Jobscan, **2025 Applicant Tracking System (ATS) Usage Report**.
- **Year:** 2025 (figure), report updated 2025.
- **URL:** https://www.jobscan.co/blog/fortune-500-use-applicant-tracking-systems/
- **Reliability:** **High.** Jobscan reverse-engineers each Fortune 500 career page
  to detect the ATS actually deployed (not survey recall). Caveat: "uses an ATS"
  is NOT "auto-rejects resumes" — see the rejected myth below. Safe phrasing:
  "~98% of Fortune 500 companies use an applicant tracking system." Do NOT say
  "98% of resumes are auto-rejected."
- **Display value:** `98%` (round 97.8% → "nearly all"; "98%" is defensible given
  the 3-year band). Source label: "Jobscan, 2025".

### 2. 62% of hiring managers: an AI-generated resume WITHOUT customization is more likely to be rejected  — **[2024–2026]**

- **Claim:** Generic, uncustomized applications get rejected — the rejection
  trigger is lack of tailoring, not AI use itself. This is the single most
  on-message recent stat for ResumeTailor: it says *tailoring is what saves you.*
- **Number:** **62%** say AI-generated resumes without personalization are more
  likely to be rejected. (Companion finding: 78% say personalized details signal
  genuine interest/fit.)
- **Source:** Resume Now, **"2025 AI and the Applicant Report"** — survey of **925
  US HR professionals**, fielded March 28, 2025.
- **Year:** **2025.**
- **URL:** https://www.resume-now.com/job-resources/careers/ai-applicant-report
- **Reliability:** **Medium-high.** Disclosed sample size (925) and field date;
  self-reported attitudes, not observed behavior. Vendor-run (Resume Now sells
  resume tools), so mild interest bias toward "you must customize." The finding
  is directional and well-aligned with the broader, independent ResumeGo field
  data on tailoring (#3, #4). Phrase as reported: "62% of hiring managers say a
  generic, un-customized AI resume is more likely to be rejected (Resume Now,
  2025)." Do NOT round to "AI resumes get rejected" — the point is *uncustomized*.
- **Display value:** `62%`. Source label: "Resume Now, 2025".

### 3. A tailored cover letter drove a 53% higher callback rate  — **[older, still valid]**

- **Claim:** A cover letter written for the specific job measurably beats both a
  generic letter and no letter — directly validates the cover letter in our kit.
- **Numbers:** **16.4% callback** with a job-specific (tailored) cover letter vs
  **12.5%** with a generic cover letter vs **10.7%** with none → tailored is
  **53% higher** than no cover letter (and beats a generic one).
- **Source:** ResumeGo field experiment — **7,287 fictitious applications** to real
  openings on Indeed, ZipRecruiter, Glassdoor, **July 15, 2019 – Jan 10, 2020**.
- **Year:** 2020 (published; data 2019–2020).
- **URL:** https://www.resumego.net/research/cover-letters/
- **Reliability:** **High for an industry source.** Large field experiment with a
  control group and disclosed window/sample. Vendor-run but methodology is the
  most transparent in this space. Honest phrasing: "Applications with a *tailored*
  cover letter had a 53% higher callback rate than applications with none, in a
  7,287-application field experiment (ResumeGo)." Do not generalize beyond "cover
  letter callback rate."
- **Display value:** `53%`. Source label: "ResumeGo field study, 2020".

### 4. A comprehensive / effortful application drove 71% more callbacks  — **[older, still valid]**

- **Claim:** Putting real effort into matching your application to the role
  measurably increases interview callbacks; a thin, generic application does not.
- **Numbers:** **13.5% callback** for resumes linking a comprehensive LinkedIn
  profile vs **7.9%** with no profile → **71% higher**. (A "bare-bones" profile
  got 7.2% — i.e., no lift; *effort* is what moves the number.)
- **Source:** ResumeGo field experiment — **24,570 fictitious resumes** to real
  openings on Indeed, ZipRecruiter, Glassdoor, **Oct 9, 2018 – Mar 8, 2019**.
  Reported by Fortune; charted by Statista.
- **Year:** 2019.
- **URL:** https://www.resumego.net/research/linkedin-interview-chances/
- **Reliability:** **High for an industry source.** Large, randomized field
  experiment, variables held constant. Caveat: measures *profile comprehensiveness/
  effort*, not literally "tailoring a resume." Honest phrasing: "Putting real
  effort into your application drove 71% more callbacks in a 24,570-resume field
  study (ResumeGo)." Do NOT restate as "tailored resumes get 71% more callbacks."
- **Display value:** `71%`. Source label: "ResumeGo field study, 2019".

### 5. Recruiters spend ~7 seconds on the initial resume scan  — **[older, still valid]**

- **Claim:** A resume gets only seconds of attention, so relevance to *this* role
  must be obvious at a glance — which is what tailoring delivers.
- **Number:** **7.4 seconds** average initial screening time.
- **Source:** Ladders eye-tracking study (2018 update of its 2012 study), widely
  reported by HR Dive and HR Daily Advisor.
- **Year:** 2018.
- **URL:** https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/
- **Reliability:** **Medium.** Real eye-tracking study from a known company, widely
  cited, but the published report does not disclose participant count or sample, so
  treat as directional/illustrative. Cite as "about 7 seconds (Ladders, 2018)."
- **Display value:** `~7 sec`. Source label: "Ladders eye-tracking study, 2018".

Usable inside ad bodies / landing copy as flavor, with attribution. Not strong or
recent enough to be a hero stat, or methodologically softer than the set above.

- **~250 applications per corporate opening; ~4–6 interviewed, 1 hired.**
  Glassdoor HR stats; widely repeated. **[undated/aging]** — real-ish but the
  underlying date/dataset is not clearly documented and it is several years old.
  Newer triangulated figures land around **~242 applications** per opening
  (The Interview Guys, 2025, meta-analysis of multiple studies) and "~42
  applications to land one interview in 2025." Use as *context for competition*,
  not as a precise current measurement. URL:
  https://www.glassdoor.com/blog/50-hr-recruiting-stats-make-think/ ·
  https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/the-average-job-opening-now-gets-242-applications/
- **74% of US job seekers personally use AI in their application process.**
  Greenhouse 2025 Workforce & Hiring Report (survey of 1,200 US job seekers).
  **[2024–2026]** Reliable, recent — but it's about *job-seeker AI use*, not our
  value prop directly. Good for "everyone's using AI now → generic AI output is
  the new average; tailoring is the differentiator." URL:
  https://www.greenhouse.com/blog/greenhouse-2025-workforce-hiring-report
- **81% of companies use skills-based hiring (up from 73% in 2023, 56% in 2022).**
  Widely reported 2025 figure. **[2024–2026]** Supports "match the *skills/keywords*
  in the posting." Triangulated across HR stat roundups; trace to a primary
  (TestGorilla / LinkedIn skills-based hiring report) before quoting precisely.
- **Brazil — Gupy is the dominant ATS** (10,000+ employer clients incl. Nubank,
  iFood, Ambev, Magazine Luiza), scoring candidates 0–100 with its "Gaia" AI; per

  data — fine as *Brazil ATS-reality color* (the screen is real and ranks you),
  not as a hiring-outcome promise. URL: https://www.gupy.io/inteligencia-artificial

## Commonly claimed but NOT rigorously sourced — DO NOT publish as fact

Circulating widely, but debunked, unsourced, or self-referential vendor numbers.
Do not present any as cited industry fact.

### A. "75% of resumes are rejected by the ATS before a human sees them" — REJECTED / MYTH

- **Why excluded:** Traces to a **2012 sales pitch by Preptel** (a resume vendor
  that folded in 2013). No study/methodology was ever published; recruiters report
  the majority of applications are seen by a human. **Do not use this, or any
  "X% never seen by a human" variant** — in EN, pt-BR ("ATS filtra 75% dos
  currículos"), or ES. It is the single most common false claim in this space and
  citing it destroys credibility.
- **Debunk:** https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/ats-resume-rejection-myth/
- **Safe alternative:** Stat #1 ("~98% of Fortune 500 use an ATS") — true.

### B. "Tailored resumes get 18% vs 2%" / "11.7% vs 4.2%" / "40% more callbacks" / "42% more interviews, 27% higher offers" / "5.8% tailored conversion (1.6x)" — NOT RIGOROUSLY SOURCED

- **Why flagged:** These exact figures recur across resume-tool blogs (Scale.jobs,
  Resumly, onehour.digital, high5test, Hiring Thing, etc.) but each traces back to
  another blog or to unpublished internal vendor data — **no reproducible,
  independent study.** The *direction* ("tailoring/quantifying helps") is well
  supported; the *specific percentages* are not. The "11.7% vs 4.2% from a study
  of 15,000 applications" in particular has **no identifiable primary source.** Do
  not publish any of these as cited fact. If we want a number this strong, it must
  come from **our own measured A/B / cohort data**, labeled as our result. Until
  then, use #3 (ResumeGo 53% cover letter) and #4 (ResumeGo 71%), which are real
  experiments.

### C. "31% more likely to get an interview with a customized resume (ResumeGo)" — UNVERIFIED ATTRIBUTION

- **Why flagged:** Widely attributed to ResumeGo across blogs, but it does **not
  appear on ResumeGo's own research index** alongside its documented studies (the
  LinkedIn-profile 71% study and the cover-letter study). The attribution may be a
  blog conflation. **Do not cite "ResumeGo, 31%."** Use ResumeGo's two studies
  that are actually published (#3, #4) instead.

### D. "Resumes with quantifiable results get 40% more callbacks (Jobscan)" — USE WITH CAUTION

- **Why flagged:** "40% more callbacks / 40% higher chance with metrics" appears in
  many blogs attributing it to "a Jobscan study" or Glassdoor, but the specific
  report/methodology is not pinned down. **Quantifying achievements is genuinely
  good advice**, but do not cite the "40%" as a sourced number. Phrase as advice
  ("add numbers to your bullets"), not as a statistic, unless a primary is found.

### E. "10,000+ / 11,000 / 14,200 job applications submitted per minute on LinkedIn" — INCONSISTENT, ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY

- **Why flagged:** The "per minute" figure swings wildly across sources (6,060 /
  9,000 / 11,000 / 14,200), with the highest numbers in SEO listicles, not a
  stable LinkedIn primary. Use only as loose color ("you're competing with a
  flood of applicants"), never as a precise cited number.

### F. "94% of hiring managers say cover letters influence decisions" / "1.9x with tailored cover letter" — USE THE RESUMEGO FIELD DATA INSTEAD

- **Why flagged:** The "94% influence" figure is a vendor *survey* of stated
  preference (Resume Genius), and the "1.9x" appears in listicles without a
  traceable original. Prefer #3 (ResumeGo's **observed** 53% callback lift from a
  field experiment) as the defensible cover-letter fact.
